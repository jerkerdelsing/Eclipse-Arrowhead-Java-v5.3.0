\section{Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

This document describes the Onboarding system. Onboarding implements the Arrowhead 5.2.2 onboarding procedure: a device holding only a manufacturer-issued bootstrap certificate, approved by an operator, obtains its onboarding, device and system certificates from the local-cloud Certificate\allowbreak{}Authority, its ServiceRegistry device entry and the Authentication identities of its systems. Off-boarding withdraws all of it.

\subsection{Significant Prior Art}
\label{sec:prior_art}

After Bicaku et al., Interacting with the Arrowhead Local Cloud: On-boarding Procedure (2018); migration of the v4.6.2 onboarding controller.

\subsection{How This System Is Meant to Be Used}
\label{sec:use}

Operator: \texttt{approve-\allowbreak{}device} with the bootstrap fingerprint and the approved systems. Device: \texttt{onboard-\allowbreak{}with-\allowbreak{}csr}, \texttt{onboard-\allowbreak{}device}, then \texttt{onboard-\allowbreak{}system} per system, each step with the certificate of the previous one. Systems then log in, register and orchestrate. \texttt{offboard-\allowbreak{}device} (device or operator) removes everything, revokes the certificates and blacklists the systems.

\subsection{System functionalities and properties}
\label{sec:properties}

\subsubsection{Functional properties of the system}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Certificate-only enrollment with approved bootstrap certificates (SHA-256 fingerprint).
  \item Onboarding (\texttt{on}, at most 24 h), device (\texttt{de}) and system (\texttt{sy}) certificates signed by the Certificate\allowbreak{}Authority; CSR or generated key.
  \item ServiceRegistry device entry and Authentication identities.
  \item Off-boarding: registry entries, identities, revocation, blacklisting.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Configuration of system properties}

The system is configured with Spring properties (\texttt{application.properties}, overridden by environment variables in the container deployment). The properties that shape its behaviour are:

\rowcolor{gray!33} Property & Default & Meaning \\ \hline \endhead
\texttt{server.\allowbreak{}port} & 8435 & HTTPS port. \\ \hline
\texttt{authentication.\allowbreak{}policy} & outsourced & Identity tokens for generalManagement. \\ \hline
\texttt{service.\allowbreak{}interface.\allowbreak{}policy} & CERT\_AUTH & Policy of the registered interfaces. \\ \hline
\texttt{management.\allowbreak{}policy} & sysop-only & Access to generalManagement. \\ \hline
\texttt{ONBOARDING\_\allowbreak{}TLS\_\allowbreak{}*,\allowbreak{} ONBOARDING\_\allowbreak{}TRUSTSTORE*} & (required) & System certificate and the trusted cloud and manufacturer CAs. \\ \hline
\texttt{ONBOARDING\_\allowbreak{}HOST} & localhost & Advertised host. \\ \hline
\texttt{ONBOARDING\_\allowbreak{}DB\_\allowbreak{}USER} & onboarding & Database user (password from a secret file). \\ \hline

Secrets (database, keystore and identity passwords, secret keys) have no usable defaults; the standalone deployment reads them from per-deployment Compose file secrets under \texttt{/\allowbreak{}run/\allowbreak{}secrets}.

The standalone deployment of this folder uses \texttt{config/\allowbreak{}application.\allowbreak{}properties} (copied into the image) with \texttt{server.\allowbreak{}port}=8435, \texttt{authentication.\allowbreak{}policy}=\texttt{outsourced}, \texttt{service.\allowbreak{}interface.\allowbreak{}policy}=\texttt{CERT\_AUTH}, \texttt{management.\allowbreak{}policy}=\texttt{sysop-\allowbreak{}only}, \texttt{service.\allowbreak{}authorization.\allowbreak{}enabled}=\texttt{true}, \texttt{mqtt.\allowbreak{}api.\allowbreak{}enabled}=\texttt{false}, \texttt{arrowhead.\allowbreak{}ssl.\allowbreak{}crl-\allowbreak{}file}=\texttt{/\allowbreak{}crl/\allowbreak{}ca.\allowbreak{}crl}; secrets are read from Compose file secrets under \texttt{/\allowbreak{}run/\allowbreak{}secrets} (\texttt{spring.\allowbreak{}config.\allowbreak{}import=\allowbreak{}configtree:\allowbreak{}/\allowbreak{}run/\allowbreak{}secrets/}). \texttt{enable.\allowbreak{}blacklist.\allowbreak{}filter} is not enabled there, so incoming requests are not checked against the Blacklist; blacklisted providers are still removed by Dynamic\allowbreak{}Service\allowbreak{}Orchestration.

\subsubsection{Data stored by the system}

Device and system enrollments and log entries (database \texttt{ah\_\allowbreak{}onboarding}). No CA key.

\subsubsection{Non functional properties}

Each step calls the Certificate\allowbreak{}Authority, the ServiceRegistry and Authentication synchronously.

\subsubsection{Stateful or stateless}

Stateful: enrollments are persistent.

\subsection{Important Delimitations}
\label{sec:delimitations}

Onboarding does not sign certificates itself and does not approve devices; an operator does. An approval names 1 to 32 systems per device, none of them a reserved name (\texttt{ONBOARDING\_\allowbreak{}RESERVED\_\allowbreak{}NAMES}: the core and support systems, Sysop and the test clients). Only the onboarding and device certificate issued last are accepted, and only while the CA reports them GOOD.

\section{Security}
\label{sec:security}

The system can run in an insecure mode (\texttt{server.ssl.enabled=false}, plain HTTP, identity declared by the requester) for development, and in the Arrowhead secure mode with HTTPS and X.509 certificates following the Arrowhead certificate profile (\texttt{<System>.<Cloud>.<Organization>.arrowhead.eu}). Certificates not issued under the cloud CA are refused by the TLS handshake. The standalone deployment of this folder (\texttt{config/\allowbreak{}application.\allowbreak{}properties} and \texttt{compose.yaml}) runs the secure mode.

\subsection{Security model}


\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Protocols:} HTTPS (TLS 1.3 and 1.2) with mandatory client certificates; MQTT over TLS; AMQP over TLS for \texttt{sysMonitor}.
  \item \textbf{Data protection:} transport encryption by TLS; the database connection uses TLS with server verification in the standalone deployment. Secrets are kept out of the container environment (Compose file secrets).
  \item \textbf{System authentication:} by the authentication policy: \texttt{certificate}, \texttt{outsourced} (identity token verified with Authentication) or \texttt{declared} (insecure); the standalone deployment uses \texttt{outsourced}. An identity token is accepted only together with the client certificate of the system it was issued to (token-to-certificate binding), and every client certificate is checked against the cloud CA's CRL (fail closed).
  \item \textbf{Authorization of produced services:} management services are protected by the management filter (\texttt{management.policy}: \texttt{SYSOP\_ONLY}, \texttt{WHITELIST} or \texttt{AUTHORIZATION}); with \texttt{service.authorization.enabled=true} ConsumerAuthorization decides each call; an optional blacklist filter refuses blacklisted requesters. Sysop identities bypass the management filter.
  \item Every caller presents a trusted client certificate; certificates and peer IP are taken from the TLS connection (no forwarded identity headers). \texttt{onboarding\allowbreak{}Management} needs the Sysop token bound to the operator certificate.
\end{itemize}

For the Arrowhead certificate profile see github.com/eclipse-arrowhead/documentation.
